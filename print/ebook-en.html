The Global Mindset — Thinking Like a World-Class Trader

Export-import is not merely "shipping goods abroad" — it is a game of strategy that demands a new way of thinking.

1.1 The Three Pillars of the Strategy Game

PillarWhat It Means for Your Business
Market intelligenceReading other countries' needs before your competitors do — data first, intuition second.
Regulatory complianceTurning rules into a weapon, not an obstacle: whoever understands the rules moves fastest.
Reputation buildingA clean customs and payment track record is an asset that lowers your costs year after year.

1.2 Shifting Perspective: from Local to Global

Step 1 — Research market demand. Don't sell what you happen to have; sell what they need. Malaysia needs processed palm products, Japan needs energy, China needs nickel — every country has its own shopping list.

Step 2 — Understand cultural nuance. Japan values detail and punctuality; the Middle East builds relationships first, business later; Europe demands standards compliance. Culture shapes how you negotiate.

Step 3 — Build a long-term vision. One highly profitable shipment means nothing next to a ten-year partnership. Global traders build lanes, not one-off deals.

1.3 Traits of Traders Who Last

  • Adaptive — regulations change every year; the rigid get left behind.
  • Resilient — held cargo, volatile currency, buyers backing out: every successful exporter has lived through all of them.
  • Network-oriented — trade shows, associations, communities: the biggest opportunities often arrive through introductions.
  • Tech-savvy — from market research to clearance, everything is digital now.
M2B Field NotesOur fastest-growing clients are not the best capitalised — they are the most administratively disciplined. In the eyes of customs risk-management systems, tidiness = trust = green channel = lower cost.
Part I  ·  Chapter 02

HS Codes — The Secret Language of World Trade

The HS Code is your product's "DNA": it determines tariffs, permits and facilities. One wrong digit has real consequences.

2.1 Reading the Code Structure

Example 1509.10.00  →  15 Chapter (fats & oils)  →  09 Heading (olive oil)  →  10 Subheading (virgin)  →  00 national tariff line (8 digits in Indonesia's BTKI)

The first six digits are universal worldwide (WCO); the final two digits are Indonesia's national breakdown in the BTKI tariff book.

2.2 The Cost of Getting It Wrong

MistakeImpactPrevention
Code too genericHigher import duty than necessaryStudy the product's technical specification
Code doesn't match the physical goodsCargo held; potential underpayment penaltiesComplete documentation & product photos
Not tracking BTKI updatesAdministrative sanctions, repeated adjustment notesPeriodic classification reviews

2.3 How to Determine the Right HS Code

1 — Analyse the product's composition. Three key questions: what is the main material? the main function? how does it technically work? The answers determine the tariff line.

2 — Consult official databases. BTKI via insw.go.id (Indonesia NTR menu), the beacukai.go.id portal, and the WCO HS Database for international comparison.

3 — Ask the experts. An experienced customs broker (PPJK), your industry association, or apply for a pre-entry classification ruling (PKSI) from Indonesian Customs for legal certainty on goods you import routinely.

M2B Field NotesFor high-value or recurring goods, a PKSI ruling is the best investment: the classification decision is legally binding, making you immune to code debates at the port. The M2B team can help prepare the file.

Core Documents — The Heart of Operations

Three documents drive the whole transaction. Consistency between them matters more than the beauty of any single one.

3.1 The Mandatory Trio

Supporting documents: PO/sales contract, tax & business registration (NPWP & NIB), Certificate of Origin (for FTA tariffs), fumigation & phytosanitary certificates as the commodity requires.

3.2 PIB vs PEB — Two Doors, One System

3.3 Pre-Submission Checklist

  • Invoice complete: description, qty, price, terms
  • Packing list detailed per package
  • B/L or AWB issued & correct
  • Technical permits / Lartas in hand
  • CoO prepared when claiming FTA tariffs
  • Every figure consistent across documents
CautionMost red-channel assignments begin with a simple inconsistency: the packing-list weight differs from the B/L, or the invoice description doesn't match the HS Code. Cross-check the three core documents before submitting — five minutes that saves days.

Part I  ·  Chapter 05

Modern Clearance Systems

Modern clearance is proactive: the documents travel ahead of the goods.

5.1 Two Opposite Ways of Working

The old way  →  goods arrive → start preparing documents → submit → wait → panic when something goes wrong
The modern way  →  documents ready → pre-declare before the vessel berths → track in real time → quick release

5.2 Three Pillars of Implementation

Pre-arrival submission. A PIB can be lodged before the goods arrive. Complete documents up front mean channelling & payment are done early — cargo moves the moment it leaves the ship.

INSW integration. The Indonesia National Single Window (insw.go.id) unifies cross-agency permits — quarantine, trade, health — behind one online door. Submit once; every agency reads the same data.

Real-time tracking. Monitor document and cargo status through the customs CEISA portal / your broker's system. Early detection = no storage-fee surprises.

6.3 Cash Out ≠ Landed Cost: The Detail Everyone Misses

For a VAT-registered business (PKP), import VAT is creditable input tax and Article 22 income tax is a tax credit — neither is a permanent cost; they are money "parked" with the tax office.

PerspectiveValue
Total cash out (the cash you must prepare)± Rp 1,045.8 m
Effective landed cost for a PKP (basis for COGS & pricing)
= customs value + duty + local costs (VAT & Art. 22 credited)		± Rp 924.4 m

Pricing from the cash-out figure makes you uncompetitive; pricing without Article 22 and the tax FX rate destroys your margin. Compute both, and know the difference.

CautionWithout an API import licence (or NIB functioning as one), Article 22 jumps from 2.5% to 7.5% — roughly Rp 45 million extra in the example above. Complete your import legality before the goods depart.

Technical Permits & Restricted Goods (Lartas)

Lartas — prohibitions and restrictions — is the gate you must pass before your goods ever touch the port.

7.1 Four Lartas Categories

CategoryExamplesConsequence
Import prohibitionNarcotics, illegal weaponsTotally banned — criminal offence
Import restrictionTextiles, certain electronics, iron & steelImport approval / surveyor report required
Export restrictionCrude palm oil, mineral ores, timberExport permit/quota required
Two-way restrictionAgricultural & animal productsPermits both ways + quarantine

7.2 Technical Permits by Product Type

Agency/RegimeApplies ToForm of Compliance
Mandatory SNIHelmets, children's toys, electrical equipment, tyresLab test → SNI certificate → marking
BPOM (food & drug)Processed food, cosmetics, supplementsMD/ML distribution permit, cosmetic notification
QuarantinePlants, animals & their productsPhytosanitary / veterinary certificate + inspection
SDPPI/telecomTelecom devices, mobile phonesDevice certificate, IMEI registration

7.3 The No-Surprise SOP: Check Lartas Before the PO

Confirm the exact HS Code  →  open insw.go.id → Lartas Information  →  read the required permits (import & export)  →  estimate permit time & cost  →  only then issue the PO to your supplier

Realistic timelines: identifying required permits 1–3 days; preparing documents 4–7 days; agency processing 1–3 weeks depending on permit type. A permit that chases goods already shipped = storage fees you never needed to pay.

M2B Field NotesThe most common mistake we see: PO paid, goods on the water, then the owner discovers the product needs a pre-shipment surveyor report in the origin country — which must be arranged before loading. The "tuition fee" can exceed the cargo's value. Make the Lartas check your first reflex, not your last thought.
Part II  ·  Chapter 08

Handling the Red Channel

The red channel is not a guilty verdict — it is an inspection. Preparation decides the outcome.

8.1 The Most Frequent Triggers

  • HS Code not matching the physical goods
  • Pricing out of line with customs benchmark data
  • Incomplete or inconsistent documents
  • New company / compliance record not yet established
  • High-risk commodity or country of origin

8.2 During the Physical Inspection

What officers check: that goods type & quantity match the declaration, physical condition, labels/markings, and packaging.

Your RightsYour Obligations
Be present/represented at inspectionCooperate and respond promptly
Ask the basis of the inspectionProvide labour & tools to open packaging
Object to an assessmentKeep the goods secure throughout

8.3 Mitigation Before & After

  1. Document before shipping — photos of goods, packaging, and container stuffing.
  2. Pre-shipment inspection for high-value cargo or new suppliers.
  3. Transparent communication with the exporter — make sure what ships = what's written.
  4. Work with an experienced broker who knows the objection & appeal procedures if an assessment seems wrong.
M2B Field NotesYour attitude during inspection is on record. Importers who are cooperative, organised and responsive build a good reputation precisely through the red channel — and later shipments improve. Panic, defensiveness, or "shortcuts" have the opposite effect, and it lasts.
Part II  ·  Chapter 09

The Anti-Fraud Framework

In cross-border trade, reputation costs more than margin — and fraud hunts both.

9.1 Four Schemes You Must Recognise

SchemeHow It WorksLegal Consequence
Under-invoicingDocumented value below the real transactionCustoms criminal charges, fines, blacklist
Over-invoicingInflated value (transfer-pricing scheme)Tax audit, sanctions
MisclassificationDeliberately wrong HS Code for lower tariffPenalties, seizure
Phantom shipmentDocuments for cargo that never existedImprisonment

9.2 Red Flags — Read Before You Transfer

From suppliers: prices far below market · refusing L/C without clear reason · asking for payment to a personal account · inconsistent documents · no verifiable track record.

From buyers: unreasonable urgency · requests to alter documents · unclear company address · negative reviews from previous partners.

9.3 Best-Practice Protections

  • Segregate duties (maker–checker) in finance
  • Due diligence on every new partner
  • Structured, well-kept documentation
  • Internal audit every 6 months
  • Compliance training for the team
  • Secure payment: L/C or escrow for new partners
CautionIndependently verify any destination account (call the company's official number, not the one in the e-mail) whenever "bank details change" — business e-mail compromise is today's most common payment fraud.

Trade Facilities — Save Money the Smart Way

The government offers plenty of "official discounts". The only difference between users and non-users is knowledge.

10.1 Free Trade Agreements (FTA)

FTAPartnersKey Benefit
ACFTAASEAN–ChinaPreferential tariffs across thousands of lines
AKFTAASEAN–KoreaPreferential tariffs
IJEPAIndonesia–JapanMany automotive & industrial lines at 0%
AIFTAASEAN–IndiaAccess to South Asia's giant market

The key to claiming FTA tariffs: a correctly issued and completed Certificate of Origin (or origin declaration), plus meeting the Rules of Origin. Wrong form = normal tariff applies.

10.2 KITE — Import Facility for Export Purposes

KITE Exemption: imported raw materials enter free of duty & import taxes, provided the output is exported within a set period. KITE Refund: duty already paid is refunded once exports are proven. Ideal for export-oriented manufacturers, including small industry.

Incoterms 2020 — Where Risk & Cost Change Hands

Three letters in a contract decide who pays for what — and who eats the loss when something goes wrong.

Incoterms (International Commercial Terms, published by the ICC and revised every decade) govern three things: where responsibility transfers, how costs are split, and where the risk of damage/loss passes. Always write the version into the contract: "CIF Tanjung Priok, Incoterms® 2020".

17.1 The 11-Term Risk Map — Understand at a Glance

17.2 Choosing the Right Term

Your SituationPickWhy
New importer, little experienceCIF / DAPSeller arranges carriage; you focus on clearance
Importer wanting logistics & cost controlFOB / FCAYou pick the forwarder & negotiate freight yourself
New exporterFOBResponsibility ends once goods are on board
Exporter protecting a VIP buyer's experienceCIF / DDPFull service, larger service margin

17.3 Common Mistakes

MistakeImpactFix

International Payment Methods

The golden rule: the better you know your partner, the simpler the method.

18.1 Five Methods in One Table

MethodSeller SecuritySpeedCostBest For

18.2 The Letter of Credit Flow

The core principle of an L/C: banks pay on document compliance, not cargo condition. One letter differing from the L/C terms = a discrepancy = delayed payment. Scrutinise the terms before accepting.

18.3 Variants & Safeguards

L/C types: Irrevocable (standard) · Confirmed (guaranteed by a second bank — for risky countries) · Transferable (for trading companies) · Revolving (repeat contracts) · Standby (backup guarantee).

T/T variants: in advance (safe for the seller) · after B/L copy (balanced) · 30/60/90-day terms (safe for the buyer).

Escrow: funds held by a third party, released after the buyer confirms — practical for online deals (e.g. trade assurance on B2B platforms).

L/C Red FlagsSoft clauses impossible to fulfil (e.g. documents must be signed by the buyer's representative) · unrealistic shipment windows · vague document requirements · a disreputable issuing bank. When in doubt, request a Confirmed L/C through your own bank.

18.4 A Quick Decision Tree

New / unknown partner  →  L/C (Confirmed if the country is risky)  ·  Known but not long-standing  →  D/P or T/T after B/L  ·  Long-trusted partner  →  T/T / open account

19.2 Claim Procedure — A Tight Timeline

0–24 hours: photos/video from every angle before anything is moved · record time-location-condition · notify carrier, forwarder & insurer · gather B/L, invoice, packing list.

24–72 hours — written Notice of Claim: incident date, vessel/flight name, damage description & quantity, estimated loss, evidence attached.

Days 3–14 — survey: the insurer's surveyor inspects; accompany them and supply whatever documents they request.

Days 14–60 — settlement: approved in full → payment; partial → negotiate with data; rejected → appeal with additional evidence.

19.3 Cheap Prevention

Layered packaging: inner protection (bubble/foam) → outer (double-wall carton / crate) → climate control (silica gel, VCI for metals, insulation for temperature-sensitive cargo). Clear labelling: This Way Up · Keep Dry · Fragile · Temperature Sensitive · Do Not Stack.

M2B Field NotesPhotos of the stuffing process (loading into the container) are "free insurance": they prove the goods' condition & loading method at departure, and have repeatedly been the deciding factor in paid claims.
Part IV  ·  Chapter 20

ISPM 15 — Wood Packaging Standards

A wooden pallet without a stamp looks trivial — until an entire container is refused entry at destination.

ISPM 15 is the international standard for treating wood packaging (pallets, crates, dunnage) to prevent pests spreading across borders. It applies in virtually every major export destination.

20.1 Materials: Required vs Exempt

MaterialISPM 15?Notes
Solid wood (pallets, crates, dunnage)REQUIREDMust be treated & marked
PlywoodExemptProduction process already kills pests
Chipboard / OSB / MDFExemptEngineered wood is excluded
Presswood palletsExemptCheap option for one-way shipments

20.2 Reading the Mark

[IPPC logo]  ID-012-HT  →  ID = country (Indonesia)  ·  012 = producer registration  ·  HT = treatment (HT heat 56°C/30 min at core · MB methyl-bromide fumigation · DH dielectric heating)

The mark must be visible, legible and permanent — on two opposite sides. Hand-painted or smudged stamps = treated as absent.

20.3 Consequences at Destination

CountryTreatment of Violations
United StatesEntry refused — re-export or destruction
European UnionDetention, mandatory treatment at shipper's cost
AustraliaStrict inspection; destruction if non-compliant
JapanRejection & return to origin
CautionISPM 15 compliance costs pocket change per pallet from a certified vendor. Non-compliance costs thousands of dollars in demurrage + re-export + your reputation with the buyer. For one-way shipments, consider presswood/plastic pallets and skip the issue entirely.

Non-Fraud Risk Mitigation — Politics & Currency

Two risks you cannot stop — but can fully manage.

22.1 Political & Regulatory Risk

RiskFormMitigation
Tariff changesSudden duty hikes in the destination countryMarket diversification; price-adjustment clauses
Embargoes/sanctionsTrade bans between statesMonitor news; avoid concentration in risky countries
Political instabilityConflict, regime changePolitical-risk insurance; safe payment terms
New regulationNew environmental/safety standardsFollow industry associations & the local embassy

Three protective habits: country due diligence (international institutions' reports, embassy briefs) · never hang > 40% of revenue on one country · make sure contracts carry force-majeure & price-adjustment clauses.

22.2 Currency Risk — The Friend That Betrays

A weakening rupiah rewards exporters and punishes importers; a strengthening one does the reverse. A 5% move can wipe out the whole margin of a thin deal — so currency exposure must be calculated, not felt.

22.3 Four Hedging Instruments

InstrumentHow It WorksPros / Cons
ForwardLock today's rate for a future-dated transactionCertain & simple / bound even if rates move your way
FuturesLike forwards, exchange-traded with leverageLiquid / large leveraged-loss risk
OptionsThe right (not obligation) to exchange at a set rateProtection + upside kept / premium cost
SwapExchange currency flows with a bankEfficient for regular two-way needs

22.4 Implement in Stages

  1. Measure exposure: what is your quarterly FX transaction value? Simulate a 5–10% move against your profit.
  2. Start simple: forward contracts through your foreign-exchange bank for transactions already locked in.
  3. Level up as needed: options/swaps for more complex profiles — with a financial adviser at your side.
M2B Field NotesHedging is not speculation — its goal is to make your margin predictable, not bigger. Exporters who hedge sleep better, and Indonesian banks run hedging assistance programmes for SMEs. Ask your foreign-exchange bank.
